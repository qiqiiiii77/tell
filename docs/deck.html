Tell

It won’t take “yes” for an answer.
A medication-adherence follow-up call that listens to how the answer was given — and refuses to write it into the record until the patient sounded like they meant it.
github.com/qiqiiiii77/tellVoice Agent API · Streaming STT v3
The problem

Ask a patient if they took
their tablets, and they say yes.

Self-reported adherence overstates the real thing by a wide margin, and every clinician who has made a follow-up call knows it.

Which is why a good nurse doesn’t listen to the word “yes”.

They listen to the half-second before it.

02Tell
What every voice agent does with that half-second

It throws it away.

Patient“Uh 900 ms yeah, yeah, I’ve been taking them.”
Transcriptyes

The record ends up holding a fact that nobody actually believes — and the next clinical decision is made on top of it.

03Tell
The one idea

The agent cannot write to the
record by itself.

It has to call a tool, and that tool is gated on how the answer sounded.

No adherence fact enters the record unless the patient sounded certain — or was asked again.

A refusal isn’t an error message. It’s a specific follow-up question, chosen by whichever signal fired, handed back to the agent in the error field of the tool result.

We control when tool.result is sent, so the gate holds the agent’s turn open until it has actually heard the patient. No race, no guessing.

05Without the gate, that record reads took_all.
How it’s built on AssemblyAI

One microphone,
two AssemblyAI sockets.

The Voice Agent API returns transcript.user as a plain string — everything an agent needs to answer a question, and nothing you need to judge whether someone meant it.

Streaming v3 returns the same speech as words with millisecond boundaries and a confidence each. One socket runs the conversation; the other measures it.

06Tell
What the gate measures

Eight signals, combined with a noisy-OR —
so one loud tell isn’t averaged away by seven quiet ones.

SignalWhat it catches
onsetDelayhow long before they answered at all
preAnswerPausedead air — gaps and drawled fillers
hedging“I think”, “pretty much”, “I try to”
fillerLoad“uh”, “um”, weighted
wordConfidencethe transcriber’s own uncertainty
revisionstarted one answer, switched to another
arousalpitch and loudness off their baseline
brevitya one-word answer that took a long time
Every signal is scored against this patient, earlier in this same call.

A patient who opens every sentence at 2.2 seconds isn’t penalised for taking 2.2 seconds. A patient who’s been answering in 300 ms and suddenly takes 2.2 is.

Ordinary turns — the small talk, the consent — are what build that baseline. Before there’s enough of them, scoring falls back to absolute thresholds and is labelled as weaker evidence, in the UI and in the record.

09Tell
Where it stops, and what it isn’t

Designed to be refused

  • One re-ask, then it accepts. A third time is an interrogation, not care — it records and flags for a clinician instead.
  • The patient is never told this exists. No mention of pauses, tone or scores. The prompt forbids it.
  • Hesitation is not deception. A low score means this answer isn’t reliable enough to act on — a claim about evidence, not about the person.

Honest limits

  • No clinical validation. Every number here comes from RAVDESS actors and synthesised speech. No real patient has used it.
  • Accent, age, stammer and cognitive conditions move the signals. Scoring within one call is a mitigation, not a solution.
  • A flagged answer is a prompt for a human to look — never a conclusion.
